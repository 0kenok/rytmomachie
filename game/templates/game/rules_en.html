Rules

Rythmomachie was played in European monasteries and universities from the 11th to the 17th century as a way to practise Boethian number theory. There are many historical rule sets. This one is a simplified modern version.

The board and pieces

The board has 8 × 16 squares. Each side has 24 pieces: 8 circles, 8 triangles, 7 squares and a pyramid. White's numbers are built from the evens 2, 4, 6, 8 and Black's from the odds 3, 5, 7, 9. White moves first.

  • White pyramid (91) is made of 36 + 25 + 16 + 9 + 4 + 1.
  • Black pyramid (190) is made of 64 + 49 + 36 + 25 + 16.

Movement

A piece may only move to an empty square. It cannot jump over other pieces.

PieceMove
Circle1 square diagonally
Triangleexactly 2 squares in a straight line (not diagonally)
Squareexactly 3 squares in a straight line (not diagonally)
Pyramidlike a circle, a triangle or a square

Capturing

Captures are not made by moving onto a piece. After each move, the game removes every enemy piece that the moving player's pieces now attack. The capturing pieces stay where they are.

Equality
One of your pieces could move onto the enemy's square, and both have the same value.
Assault (multiplication)
One of your pieces is in a straight line (including diagonals) with the enemy, with n empty squares between them, and your value × n = enemy value.
Ambush (addition)
Two of your pieces could both move onto the enemy's square, and their values add up to the enemy's value.
Siege
Every square directly above, below, left and right of the enemy piece holds one of your pieces. The edge of the board counts as blocked.

A pyramid counts as one piece. Its value is the sum of its layers.

Playing against the computer

Choose Against the computer when you create a game, then pick your side and a level:

  • Beginner: plays almost at random and takes a free piece about half the time.
  • Easy: looks at its move and your reply like Medium, but often picks a move that is not the best.
  • Medium: looks at its move and your reply, so it rarely leaves a piece to be captured.
  • Hard: looks three moves ahead (its move, your reply, its answer). It takes up to a few seconds per move.

Winning

When you create a game, you tick one or more of these victory conditions. If you tick several, the first one reached by either player wins:

  • De corpore: capture a set number of pieces (default 12).
  • De bonis: capture pieces whose values add up to a target (default 500).
  • Victoria magna: place three of your pieces next to each other in a line, inside the enemy's half of the board, so that their values form an arithmetic (2, 4, 6), geometric (4, 16, 64) or harmonic (3, 4, 6) progression.

You also win if you capture every enemy piece, if your opponent has no legal move left, or if your opponent resigns.
